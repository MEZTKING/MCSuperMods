# ADR 0081: Particle Studio, flow, templates and tests

## Context

[ADR 0080](0080-custom-particle-types.md) adds the kind `msm:particle`, richer particle options for `spawn_particle` and scripts, and the block component `msm:ambient_particles`. Beginners need a particle in one Create, power users every knob with a preview, the library needs templates and an example, and the behaviour needs tests. The particle itself is drawn only on the client, which CI has never run (audit L16), so this ADR also decides how much of the drawing can be tested. `app.asar` is 26.9 of its 32 MB budget after the portal batch (ADR 0079 findings), so new UI is loaded on demand. Facts and limits (K1–K16) are in `docs/PLAN-particles.md` and move into ADR 0080's findings at close.

## Decision

- **D1 A Particle Studio.** `msm:particle` elements open the Particle Studio (`studios/blocks-items/ParticleStudio.tsx`, model `particle-model.ts`), registered in the blocks-items `studios.tsx` as a `React.lazy` component inside a `Suspense` fallback, so its code is a separate chunk. Groups: **Pictures** (the frame list in order, each a project sprite or a vanilla sprite id; "Add frame", reorder, remove; "New picture" opens the texture editor at 8 by 8; "Split a strip into frames" takes one PNG (a horizontal or vertical strip, or a grid with a frame count) and writes the frames as `textures/particle/<id>_<n>.png` in one undoable asset write ([ADR 0045](0045-undoable-asset-writes.md)) and fills the list; "Animation" as "Once over its life", "Loop" (with "Ticks per frame") or "A random frame"), **Life and size** ("Lifetime" as one number or "Between" two, "Size", "Size at the end"), **Movement** ("Gravity" with "Floats up", "None", "Falls" shortcuts, "Drag", "Stops on blocks", "Spin"), **Colour and light** ("Tint", "Tint at the end", "Opacity", "Fade" as "None", "Out", "In and out", "Glows in the dark", "See-through", "Show from far away") and a **Preview**: a 2D canvas that plays the particle from the element's pictures (vanilla sprites through the existing vanilla texture source when the client jar is known, else a placeholder) with a "Burst" and a "Stream" mode, a dark and a light background, and the simulation of `particle-sim.ts`, the TypeScript twin of the runtime's `ParticleLook` (D6). Every change is one undoable command. Missing project frame pictures are reported in Problems by the app (`blocks-items.particle-texture-missing`, warning).
- **D2 Editing where particles are used.** The Block Studio gains a lazy "Ambient particles" section (`AmbientParticlesSection.tsx`) for `msm:ambient_particles` on every type but `portal` ("Particle", "Chance per tick", "Count", "Where" with "Centre", "Top", "Whole block", "Velocity", "Only when" a state matches); the events editor's "Spawn particles" action gains "Kind" ("A particle", "Coloured dust", "Block pieces", "Item pieces") with the matching fields (colour and size; a block; an item), "Motion" and "Show from far away", and its particle picker lists the project's particles first. The Effect Studio's and the Portal section's particle pickers list project particles (they already take any id).
- **D3 Creator flow "Particle"** (group `magic`, card "Your own particle.", `supports: hasFabric`; a starter with `creator` under [ADR 0043](0043-creator-guided-flows.md) and the `meta.creator` reopen rule): **Name**; **Look** ("Spark", the default, "Orb", "Puff", "Leaf", or "My own picture" (an `image` parameter, one frame)); **Colour** (`#ffd060`); **Size** (Small 0.1, Medium 0.2 by default, Large 0.4); **How long** (Short 10, Medium 20 by default, Long 40 ticks); **Movement** ("Floats up", the default, "Drifts", "Falls"); **Glows** (on); **Fades out** (on); **Show it** ("Around a new block", the default, which also writes a simple block with `msm:ambient_particles`; "When an item is used", which writes an item with an `on_use` `spawn_particle` burst; "Only the particle (use it with /particle)"). The Look choices are library placeholder patterns drawn four frames each (`particle_spark`, `particle_orb`, `particle_puff`, `particle_leaf`, tinted by the flow), so the default creates with zero problems on both targets.
- **D4 Templates.** Blocks-items, category "Particles" (vanilla sprites only, never bundled): "Glowing spark" (`minecraft:glow`, emissive, floats), "Falling petal" (`minecraft:cherry_0` to `_2`, random frame, falls, spins; verify the sprite names at implementation against the target's vanilla particle descriptions), "Smoke puff" (`minecraft:generic_7` to `_0`, age, fades out, rises), "Block with ambient particles" (a block with `msm:ambient_particles` of `minecraft:end_rod`). Resourcepack: "Retexture a vanilla particle" (`minecraft:particle` for `minecraft:flame`, pointing at a project sprite). Five templates.
- **D5 Example** (library): "Firefly jar": the particle `firefly_glow` (loop animation, emissive, floats, fades in and out), a jar block with `msm:ambient_particles` of it, and a "Spark wand" item whose `on_use` sends a burst with `motion` and a coloured dust ring through an options object. It passes the library test on both targets and its vanilla data pack loads on vanilla servers.
- **D6 Testing.** (a) **JUnit and a cross-language golden:** `ParticleLook` (frame index, size, colour, alpha, spin by age) is tested in JUnit, and `fixtures/particle-look/golden.json` (definitions, ages, expected frame, size, RGBA and spin, plus positions under gravity and friction for a particle that does not collide) is read by the runtime's JUnit and by the app's `particle-sim.ts` test, as `fixtures/geo-parity` ties the mob renderers (ADR 0072 D7). (b) **Runtime GameTests** in two new classes, `MsmParticleGameTest` (registration, the log line, load errors, `/particle` through the command dispatcher read back as a `ClientboundLevelParticlesPacket` by a packet-reading player, the packet's stream codec round trip with the server's registry access, the synced registry attribute, effects, leaves and portals naming project particles) and `MsmParticleSpawnGameTest` (`spawn_particle` with ids and options objects, `motion`, `force`, the `count` cap, an options object naming a project item, a broken options object reported once at start, `world.spawnParticle` in all forms, the ambient particle plan with a seeded random and `states`), on 26.3, 26.3 without GeckoLib and 26.4. (c) **A client route, if one works:** brief B356 tries Fabric's client GameTests (`enableClientGameTests`, `runClientGameTest`) under Xvfb with a 24-bit screen and Mesa; if a world opens, one class `MsmParticleClientGameTest` spawns each test particle with `/particle`, checks that `MsmParticle` instances exist with the expected sprite count, lifetime and layer, and saves a screenshot as a CI artefact (no pixel comparison), on both targets; if not, the outcome and the error are recorded and L16 stays as it is. (d) **A fixture** `fixtures/sample-particles` with about twelve black-box parity tests (`SampleParticlesParityTest`, packets read through `ParityEars`) on the 26.3 content jar, the 26.3 ejected jar and the 26.4 content jar, and a server check pinning the log line. (e) **An e2e path** `particles-path`. (f) The fixture README's checklist for what only eyes can check: each particle's look, animation, colour and fade, glow in the dark, collision, the ambient block particles, the wand burst and the dust ring, `force` from far away, Particles: Minimal, a resource pack retexturing flame.
- **D7 Guide and AI helper.** A new guide page `docs/guide/particles.md` (sidebar entry), linked from `blocks-and-items.md`, `effects-and-throwables.md`, `portals.md`, `scripting.md` and `creator.md`, with the limits users meet (K1, K2, K3, K6, K7, K8, K9, K12); the AI helper's system prompt gains a "Particles" paragraph (the kind, its knobs and defaults, options objects, `msm:ambient_particles`, `motion` and `force`, the Particle flow, the limits).

## Consequences

- A beginner gets a particle shown around a block in one Create; power users get every knob with a live preview; vanilla particles become usable with colours, blocks and items from actions and scripts.
- The app grows by one lazy Studio chunk, one lazy Block Studio section and four placeholder patterns, checked against the asar budget in every app and library brief.
- If B356 succeeds, the project gains its first automated client run (narrowing L16 and opening the reserve brief B123's route); otherwise the golden is the strongest check of the drawing, and the README checklist the only one of the look.

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-14), with the defaults below; the user may still veto them. Vetoable defaults: the Studio's name "Particle Studio", its groups and labels (D1); the frame file names `<id>_<n>.png` and 8 by 8 new pictures (D1); the "Kind" choices in the events editor (D2); the flow name "Particle", its card, questions and defaults (Spark, `#ffd060`, Medium, 20 ticks, Floats up, glowing, fading, shown around a new block) (D3); the five template names (D4); the "Firefly jar" example (D5); a new fixture rather than more of the sample mod, and the client GameTest attempt with screenshots kept but not compared (D6).
