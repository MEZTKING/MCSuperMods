# ADR 0092: Minecraft-style art

## Context

The user asked for much better art "rather than using your own art styles", and for the best method to get Minecraft-looking art. Today every starter, Creator flow, Surprise me result and example uses the placeholder patterns of `@msm/plugin-library` (`placeholderPng`, ADR 0020 D3): flat one-colour shapes with a bevel. They are original and byte-stable, but they do not look like vanilla: no hue-shifted ramps, no clustered texture, thin outlines, small heads on tools.

Two constraints frame the answer. MSM never bundles Mojang assets; it reads them from the user's client jar at run time ([ADR 0011](0011-client-jar-asset-extraction.md)), and ADR 0011 also says nothing from that cache is ever written to an export. Picture to texture ([ADR 0048](0048-pictures-screenshots-and-skins.md)) already turns any picture into a texture, but it does not make a picture look vanilla.

A prototype of two approaches lives in `packages/plugins/library/src/art/` (exported as `minecraftArt`), with unit tests on synthetic textures only. Comparison pictures were made outside the repository from the 26.3 client jar in the Loom cache, for the user's decision. Nothing is wired into a flow.

## Decision

The user chose on 2026-11-21: **option B, the original vanilla-style generator** (D2, the default this ADR proposed). **Option A (D1) is not chosen.** D1, D3, D4 and D5 below are kept as the record of what was offered; D6 onwards are the main session's decisions for B, planned in [`PLAN-art.md`](../PLAN-art.md) (B491–B502).

- **D1 A: recolour from the user's own textures, for local use. Not chosen (user, 2026-11-21).** `recolourTexture` takes a vanilla texture from the user's extracted cache (a diamond ore, sword or helmet), finds the material against its base (the ore's stone, the same tool in iron) by palette membership, or by two-cluster colour clustering when there is no base, and maps each material colour to a ramp of the target colour with the same number of shades and the same lightness steps, hue-shifted (warmer highlights, cooler shadows). Alpha is kept, and an animated strip is recoloured with one map so its `.mcmeta` is reused unchanged. The results are derived from Mojang's textures:
  - they are offered only when a client jar is found, and are made on the user's machine at the user's request;
  - each texture made this way is marked as derived in the project (`meta.derivedFrom: "minecraft:<path>"` on the asset entry; verify at implementation where asset metadata lives);
  - export and publishing (modpack upload, Share, the docs site) warn before including derived pictures: "These pictures are recoloured from Minecraft's own textures. They are fine in your own game; do not publish them." with "Replace with generated art" (D2) as the default button;
  - this amends ADR 0011's "nothing from the cache is ever written to an export" for derived pictures only, and only with the warning; the cache itself is still never exported or packaged.
- **D2 B: an original vanilla-style generator, the default. Chosen (user, 2026-11-21); D6 to D17 make it concrete.** `generateArt(recipe, { colour, seed, finish, leafColour, style })` draws on 16x16 with original silhouettes and rules: four to six shades per material from `generateRamp`, light from the top left, clustered noise (blurred and ranked white noise, wrapping so blocks tile), a dark outline on items and none on full blocks, and a structure per material (veins in stone or deepslate, a bevel and glints or plates on blocks, boards with joints, bark streaks, growth rings, foliage with gaps). Recipes: ore, deepslate ore, gem, ingot, block (gem or metal finish), sword, pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, fruit, pie, planks, log, log top, leaves. Output depends only on recipe, colours and seed. B becomes the art of Surprise me, the Creator flows and the library examples, replacing the placeholder patterns recipe by recipe; patterns without a recipe keep their placeholder until one is drawn.
- **D3 Learned style numbers, optional. Not taken (D6).** `learnStyle` may read the user's jar at run time and keep two numbers only (cluster blur from the mean run length, hue shift from the median ramp turn), never pixels. Without a jar the built-in tuned constants apply (`DEFAULT_STYLE`, original values). The prototype's measurement over 23 vanilla textures gave a blur of 1 (the default) and a hue turn below the default, so the default hue shift may come down at implementation.
- **D4 C: an optional AI image provider. Covered by [ADR 0093](0093-ai-pictures.md) (AI pictures, implemented and Proposed); D15 ties its clean-up to B's ramps.** With the user's own key (like the AI helper, ADR 0041), a picture from an image model goes through B's clean-up before it is used: Picture to texture's crop and scale (ADR 0048), then quantising to a B ramp of the user's colour, hardening alpha and adding B's outline rule for items. Off by default; the provider, its terms and the key stay the user's; nothing is sent without the user asking.
- **D5 D: a commissioned original library. Not planned; stays the user's decision (D7 keeps recipes swappable).** A set of hand-drawn textures by a pixel artist, licensed to MSM (CC0 or MIT-compatible), recoloured at run time with D1's ramp maps (they are ours, so no warning). This costs money and time and is the user's decision; B's recipes are written so a drawn silhouette can replace a generated one without other changes.

### Option B in full (main session, 2026-11-21)

- **D6 What goes with A.** A's code leaves the library: `art/recolour.ts`, `materialMask`, `materialColours`, `textureRamps` and `learnStyle` (`art/style.ts`) are deleted, with their tests (git history keeps them). ADR 0011 stays as it is: nothing from the client jar cache reaches an export, and no MSM code reads the jar's textures for art, not even for numbers (D3 is not taken). `extractPalette` and `colourCount` stay as test helpers for the pixel rules (D13). The measured numbers of the prototype (a cluster blur of 1 and a hue turn below 16 degrees over 23 textures, DEVLOG 2026-11-14) are the only thing ever taken from vanilla, and only as two numbers already in the record.
- **D7 What the generator draws.** One recipe per kind of picture, each with an info record: `id`, a label in words ("Sword", "Planks", "Leaves"), the picture kind (`block`, `item`, `entity`, `icon`, `particle`), the size, the frame count, how many colours it takes (a main colour and, for some, a second colour), its finishes, whether it tiles and whether it has an outline. Families:
  - **Blocks** (16 by 16, tiling, no outline): plain, stone, polished stone, bricks, pillar side and top, ore and deepslate ore (stone or deepslate finish), storage block (gem, metal or rough finish), furnace-like front, bark, log top, stripped log and its top, planks, door top and bottom, trapdoor, leaves, sapling, crop stages 0 to 3, portal surface and portal frame, and terrain for Try it (dirt, grass top and side, sand, gravel, cobblestone).
  - **Items** (16 by 16, transparent, outlined): gem, ingot, disc, spawn egg (main and spot colour), the five tools, spear, mace, knife, boomerang, ball, bow and its three pulling frames, crossbow and its five states, shield, bottle, flask, totem, smithing template, horse armour and wolf armour items, the four armour pieces, fruit, pie, seeds, produce.
  - **Worn and mob pictures**: armour layers 1 and 2 (64 by 32) and horse (64 by 64) and wolf (64 by 32) body armour layers drawn from the same ramps as the armour items; box skins from a model's box-UV cubes (each face shaded by its side, a coat of `fur`, `scales`, `smooth`, `stone` or `slime`, eyes on `face` boxes, the leather saddle), and an even coat for vanilla mob variant textures, whose layout is not ours to know (plan limit A3).
  - **Icons and particles**: the effect icon (18 by 18) and the four particle pictures (8 by 8, four frames: spark, orb, puff, leaf).
  Every one of the 70 `PlaceholderPattern`s maps to a recipe; paintings (`paintingPng`, any size) keep their placeholder landscape (plan limit A2). Silhouettes are MSM's own drawings (`art/sprites.ts` and successors); a drawn silhouette can replace one without other changes (D5).
- **D8 Palettes and shading rules.** Every material is a ramp from `generateRamp`: four or five shades on items (plus the outline), five or six on blocks; light comes from the top left; highlights lean warm and shadows cool by the style's hue shift (16 degrees today; B491 may lower it to between 10 and 14 by eye on the contact sheet, recorded in the plan's notes); lightness stays between 0.12 and 0.97 (no pure black or white); shade clusters come from blurred, ranked noise with a blur of 1, wrapping on tiling blocks; items carry a one-pixel outline darker than their darkest shade in the material's hue, full blocks none; handles share one wood ramp; alpha is hard (0 or 255) everywhere except particle pictures and the portal surface, which use at most three fixed alpha levels. A recipe uses at most 12 colours on a block and 10 on an item (not counting transparent). Box skins: top faces one shade lighter, bottom faces one darker, the side faces (east and west) one darker than the front.
- **D9 How a user picks a look.** A look is `{ recipe, colour, accent?, finish?, detail, seed }`, `detail` being `smooth`, `normal` (default) or `rough` (how much shade noise). Creator flows keep their colour questions: the flow's colour drives every picture it makes, so nothing new is asked. The **Draw a picture** dialog (title "Draw it for me" in Very easy) asks **What is it?** (recipe tiles in words with a thumbnail, filtered to the picture kind and size of the field), **Colour** (a colour well, the ten Surprise me theme swatches of ADR 0086 D2 and "The thing's colour" when the field knows it), **Second colour** where the recipe has one, **Finish**, **Detail**, and shows six variations (six seeds) with **New looks** and **Use this picture**. Very easy shows only Colour and the six variations, with the recipe chosen from the slot. Nothing is stored in the project but the PNG.
- **D10 Where it plugs in.** (a) The library: `content/bundle.ts` `texture()` and the skin helpers draw through `artPng`, so every Creator flow, starter, example and Surprise me result (ADR 0086, which takes its pictures from the flows) gets drawn art in one change; flows and examples that call `placeholderPng` directly move to `artPng`. (b) A **Draw…** button (Very easy **Draw it for me**) beside **Make with AI** on every picture field ADR 0093 D6 lists: the Creator's image fields (mob skins too, drawn as box skins when the flow knows the model), the Block Studio's texture rows and slots, the Item Studio's texture rows, the Particle Studio's frames (all four frames in one step), the texture painter (replaces the canvas as one painter undo step) and **New texture…** (**Start from**: **Blank** or **Drawn picture**). (c) The Mob Studio: **Draw a skin** for a mob whose model uses box UV. (d) The AI helper (D15). (e) Try it (D16). A pick is one undo step: an asset write over an existing texture, a new asset item for a new one (ADR 0045), as Make with AI does.
- **D11 Determinism and seeds.** Output is a pure function of recipe, colours, finish, detail and seed: no `Math.random`, no clock, no locale; PNGs through the library's byte-stable `encodePng`. The library's default seed of a picture is `hashSeed(namespace, path, recipe)`, so two flows' swords differ and the same project always gets the same bytes. The dialog's six variations use seeds 0 to 5 and each **New looks** adds six. Pictures already in a project are never redrawn on their own: the switch changes what flows make from now on (plan limit A5). The ramp maths uses `Math.cbrt`, `Math.pow`, `Math.atan2`, `Math.sin` and `Math.cos`, whose last bit could differ between JavaScript engines; Node and Electron share V8, and the golden test also runs once on Windows (verify at implementation; if bytes differ, the ramps are rounded to bytes before use and goldens are compared by pixels).
- **D12 Never Mojang's.** `@msm/plugin-library` imports only `@msm/plugin-api` and `@msm/schemas`, so the generator cannot reach the client jar or `@msm/interop`; no brief opens, traces, measures or screenshots a vanilla texture to draw a recipe; silhouettes are described in words in the briefs and drawn fresh; tests, goldens and the contact sheet hold only generated pictures; comparisons with vanilla are made by the user on their own machine, never committed.
- **D13 Tests.** (a) `checkArtRules(image, info)` in `art/rules.ts`, exported for other packages' tests: size and frames; colour count caps; hard alpha (D8); outline rule (every opaque item pixel next to transparency is an outline pixel; no outline on blocks); no pure black or white; light from the top left (mean lightness of the top-left triangle above the bottom-right's); ramp order (darker shades never warmer than lighter ones of the same material); tiling (the wrap-around seams of tiling blocks differ no more than the inside). (b) Property tests over 12 colours (the ten theme colours, grey and near-white) and 4 seeds for every recipe, every finish and detail: the rules hold, the same input gives the same bytes, other seeds give other bytes, and the dominant hue is within 25 degrees of the asked colour (greys excepted). (c) Golden PNGs, one per recipe and finish at the default colour and seed 0, in `packages/plugins/library/test/fixtures/art/` (`UPDATE_GOLDENS=1`, like `fixtures/patterns/`). (d) A contact sheet `docs/reference/art/sheet.png` (every recipe at four theme colours, 4x scale) regenerated with the goldens, for review. (e) A coverage test: every `PlaceholderPattern` maps to a recipe with a drawing (no fallback left after B494). (f) The `examples/` mirror and the sample fixtures change once (B497); app unit tests for the dialog; e2e `art-path`.
- **D14 Fallback.** `artPng(pattern, options)` maps a placeholder pattern to its recipe and falls back to `placeholderPng` only for patterns with none. `placeholderPng`, its patterns and `test/fixtures/patterns/` stay unchanged, because tests, fixtures and old AI tool calls use them.
- **D15 The AI helper and AI pictures.** `create_texture` gains `recipe` (any recipe id; `pattern` still accepted and mapped by D14), `accent`, `finish` and `seed`; the app draws with the generator; its description stops calling the result a placeholder ("drawn by MSM in a Minecraft-like style; the user can redraw or paint it"). The system prompt lists the recipes. The tool catalogue's pinned hash changes once. AI pictures' clean-up (ADR 0093 D4 step 4) quantises each hue cluster to a ramp from `generateRamp` with the same shade count and rank order, so AI and drawn pictures share one palette rule. No picture-drawing tool beyond `create_texture` (ADR 0093 D9 unchanged).
- **D16 Try it.** Without the user's client jar, Try it draws vanilla blocks of a ground with the terrain and wood recipes (stone, dirt, grass, sand, gravel, cobblestone, planks, logs, leaves) in MSM's own colours instead of flat cubes; water and lava stay as they are (ADR 0087, limit L7). They are MSM's pictures, not vanilla's, and the side panel keeps saying so.
- **D17 Size.** The generator adds code only (no pictures) to the renderer; each app brief reports `node tools/check-size/index.ts` (renderer 16.2 of 18 MB before this batch). Drawing one 16 by 16 recipe stays under 2 ms on CI, six variations under 20 ms.

## Consequences

- The default art looks closer to vanilla without any Mojang bytes in the repository or the app, and the same rules cover flows, Studios, the AI helper, AI pictures and Try it.
- Example, starter and fixture PNGs change once when B497 lands (about 376 in the `examples/` mirror, the flow-built pictures of the sample fixtures, and their pinned jar hashes).
- Placeholder patterns remain the fallback and stay byte-stable, so no flow loses a picture during the rollout and old tests keep their inputs.
- A's local recolouring is gone; anyone who wants the exact vanilla look paints or imports their own pictures (Picture to texture, ADR 0048).

## Status
